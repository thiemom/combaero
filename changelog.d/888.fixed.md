- **A `ChannelElement`'s end state at a flow boundary is the duct's own (#888).** Where a channel ends on a `PressureBoundary` or `MassFlowBoundary`, `mach_in` / `mach_out` and the inlet reference block (`velocity`, `Re`, `rho_ref`, `mu_ref`, `Pr_ref`, ...) used to be read at the boundary's state: a reservoir (P = Pt, T = Tt) at an inflow, the static exit pressure with the arriving Tt at an outflow.
  - **Before:** in a 1 mm duct from a reservoir (Tt 808 K, Pt 243 kPa) to P/Pt 0.6, `mach_in` was 0.577 and `mach_out` 0.962, where the duct carries Mach 0.895 end to end. At P/Pt 0.537 the unchoked duct reported `mach_out` 1.085. Eight suction-side segments of the C3X vane example reported `mach_out` > 1.
  - **Now:** a compressible duct reports the Fanno station at that end's Mach, so `mach_in` / `mach_out` equal `M_in_duct` / `M_exit_duct` there (0.895 / 0.896, and 0.984 / 0.995 at P/Pt 0.537). An incompressible duct reports the residual's own density state (Tt at the approach static P) where the boundary holds a total pressure. The second-law check reads these Mach numbers, so its `sigma_norm` moves at such ends.
  - **Unchanged:** every residual and Jacobian (hash-identical over 560 stress networks), every solution, `T_aw` and `htc` (the correlation was already at the duct's inlet static state, #636), interior ends, `P_in` ... `Tt_out`, ribbed and pin-fin surfaces, and `CrossflowSegmentElement`.
