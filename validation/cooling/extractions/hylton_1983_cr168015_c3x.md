   - **Point 29's y is misprinted.** The page prints 0.4115 cm, read again at
     400 dpi. Its own inch value (0.0162 in = 0.0411 cm) and the 0.60 cm point

     value in the note.
   *What turns on it:* every surface position, wall thickness and passage width.

   - **The column headed "Pa" is not in pascals.** Every row equals psia x 133.3:

     `PT1_Pa_printed` keeps the printed number. `PT1_kPa_from_psia` is the
     value to use, and it agrees with M1 and Re1.
3. **Appendix A, runs 149 and 158** (PDF p. 191-192 and p. 201-202): per surface

## What was checked

- **Two-unit tables** (Table III, Table IV, Fig. 7): the cm column against the
  inch column, |cm/2.54 - in| <= 1e-4 in.
- **Coolant tables:** F to K, lbm/s to kg/s, and Re_D recomputed from the flow
  and D with Sutherland air. They agree.
- **Surface positions.** Each Appendix A row prints both s/arc and x/c_ax. Our
  arc-length parametrisation of Table III, from the minimum-x leading-edge
  point with Table IV's arcs, reproduces x/c_ax at all 176 rows: mean +0.0008,
  worst 0.0109 (`c3x_station_check.csv`).

## Derived geometry (`derive_geometry.py`, reproducible)

- **Hole centres** (`c3x_cooling_holes_xy.csv`). Fig. 7's (U, V) are mapped to
  Table III's (x, y): V runs along the pressure-side bitangent through points
  71 and 39, and U is normal to it. The profile's extent along that bitangent
  is 14.512 cm, against Table IV's true chord of 14.493.
- **Minimum wall thickness per hole and side** (`c3x_wall_thickness.csv`).
- **Passage width, area and Dh along the suction side** (`c3x_passage_geometry.csv`).
  - The width is the shortest distance to the neighbouring vane, not a true
    orthogonal width.
  - Past the throat the passage is uncovered, so the width there is only a
    crude bound.
  - **The computed throat is 3.385 cm, 2.8 % above Table IV's 3.292.** No
    correction is applied; this difference is the geometry's uncertainty.

## What the report's h is, and is not

**The tabulated external h is not measured.** The report infers it with a 2-D
finite-element conduction model of the vane, driven by the measured midspan Tw.
Its hole-side boundary condition is a correlation, Nu = Cr 0.022 Pr^0.5
Re^0.8, with Cr per hole in Fig. 7.

Feeding that h back in to predict Tw is therefore circular. **The measured
quantities are Tw, Ps and the coolant flows and temperatures.** Table VI gives
the uncertainty of the inferred h by surface region (Kline-McClintock): 6-9 %
over most of the vane, rising to 20-24 % at the trailing edge, where the wall
is thinnest.

The report prints no conductivity for the ASTM 310 stainless steel vanes;
`ss310` (Gen3 CSP, #881) covers 260-800 C.
